Gene Ontology enrichment

Define your foreground (proteins of interest) and a background (reference set). Then choose how strict the propagation should be, and run the analysis.

What is the Foreground?

The foreground is the list of UniProt IDs you are interested in: for example, proteins that are differentially expressed in your experiment, candidates from a pathway, or hits from a screen. OrthoGather looks up their GO annotations and (optionally) expands the set to their orthologs across the species you analysed.

Tip: paste UniProt accession IDs, one per line or space-separated (e.g. P0A6F5, P00350). FASTA headers are also accepted.

What is the Background?

The background is the reference universe against which enrichment is tested. A meaningful background represents "every protein that could have been picked". For most users, the easiest choice is "Use all GOA files": the full annotated proteome of every species in your analysis.

When to paste a custom list: if your experiment was restricted to a specific subset (e.g. only membrane proteins, only kinases), paste those IDs to avoid inflating enrichment.

Analysis settings Stringency choices: defaults are sensible. Adjust if you know what you want.
Evidence quality
GO annotations carry an evidence code. All includes IEA (Inferred from Electronic Annotation): the bulk of annotations in any GOA file, but the lowest quality. Curated keeps human-curated entries plus PAINT-derived IBA: a good default if you can spare some recall. Experimental only is the safest choice when you have well-annotated model organisms; expect fewer hits but with strong wet-lab support.
Orthogroups
Single-copy orthogroups have exactly one protein per species. These are the safest orthology relationships: no risk of conflating paralogs with orthologs. For comparative-functional studies across model organisms, this is a defensible default. For exploratory analyses or when including non-model species, "All orthogroups" gives more recall.
Counting
In "per protein" mode, an orthogroup with 10 members contributes 10 observations to the enrichment statistic. This can inflate significance (pseudoreplication). "Per orthogroup" collapses each OG to a single observation: more conservative p-values, but tighter against false positives. Useful when many species share large families.
